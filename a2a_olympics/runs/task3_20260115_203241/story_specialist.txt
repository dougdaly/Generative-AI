Chapter 1

The rain beat down like a drumroll in a jazz club, filling the air with the smell of wet asphalt and regret. Sam Slate leaned against the doorway of his cramped office, nursing a burning cigarette and a hand that wouldn't quit shaking. He squeezed it tight, feeling the sting of his injured left hand as he flicked the matchbook lid open, revealing "BLUE LAGOON" in faded ink. So much for a leisurely night; work had come knocking with a body.

The victim was Lionel Crane, a man whose life revolved around the intricate gears of timepieces. The word was that he was found dead inside his own walk-in safe, a bizarre twist that even a cheap dime novel wouldn’t dare spin. The door was locked from the outside, leaving behind nothing but shadows and unanswered questions. 

I followed the trail of smoke to the scene, a dilapidated workshop filled with ticking clocks and a palpable tension that hung heavier than a storm cloud. The moment I stepped inside, it felt like the gears had stopped turning. 

NEW CLUE: A smear of red sealing wax near the safe hinted at more than just punctuality gone wrong.

But just when I thought I had a lead, I felt my hand tremble. Damn thing had a mind of its own, shaking like a leaf when I wasn't playing straight. I needed a drink to steady my nerves, but first, I needed the truth.

Chapter 2

The rain kept up its relentless percussion as I dug deeper into Crane’s life, rolling through dark bars and shadowy alleys. The streets buzzed with whispers; Crane was no saint. As I sipped a whiskey in a dive called The Rusty Anchor, I caught wind of a name—Captain Rourke, the harbor master with a reputation as polished as a grease-stained engine.

Rourke was known for his connections, a man who could move cargo faster than a '48 Buick on an open road. Those who crossed him learned the hard way that he didn’t play nicely. I strolled into his domain later that night, the smell of salt and secrecy lingering in the air. 

He offered me a smirk that could curdle milk as he talked about ships and tides, but I locked onto his left cuff, a faint swatch of red wax glued to the fabric. It was the same color as the smear I’d found by Crane's safe. 

NEW CLUE: The red wax on Rourke’s cuff proclaimed a silent alliance to whatever filthy dealings he was orchestrating.

I leaned back, scanning his face for cracks in that polished demeanor. My hand quivered, and I cursed under my breath as he struck a match, lighting a cigar. It felt all too easy to tug at that thread, but too much could unravel before my eyes.

Chapter 3

It all came together like a well-oiled watch when I pieced the puzzle in the dim light of my office. The key wasn’t the safe or even the wax; it was the damp matchbook from the scene, easy to overlook but full of implications. 

I traced the connections: Rourke had been smuggling watches—real prizes that could bring him a small fortune—but Lionel had figured him out. The red wax? Rourke’s calling card, a way to seal the deal on his shady operations. Crane had crossed him, giving Rourke a motive as strong as the currents holding the docks in place.

I pictured the scene: Crane was lured to his safe—maybe a drink was promised in exchange for a partnership. Rourke locked him in after the deed was done, leaving him to suffocate while he slipped out through a back entrance designed for quick escapes. It was a trap as much as it was a murder.

NEW CLUE: The damp matchbook was from Rourke's secret rendezvous spot—The Blue Lagoon—where the illicit dealings flowed like the whiskey.

As Rourke strolled into my office to bluff me with charm and bravado, my hand began to shake involuntarily. I knew that if the truth slipped from my lips, it would mean curtains for him. But I was ready to gamble; the stakes were too high. A smile stretched across my face as I readied the noose, and I caught his eye, and for a sliver of a moment, I could almost feel the tide changing.
