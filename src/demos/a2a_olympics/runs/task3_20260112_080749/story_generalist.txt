### Chapter 1

The rain drummed a steady rhythm against the pavement, a funeral march for a dead man whose life flickered out in the shadows. I stood outside 9th Avenue’s dingy corner watch shop, the pungent aroma of stale tobacco curling through the air as I flicked the ash off my cigarette. Lionel Crane had clocked out for the last time, an unlikely victim found in a locked walk-in safe that felt as impenetrable as sin itself.

“Slate!” barked Detective Hargrove, his voice rough as gravel. “Get in here. Get a whiff of this.”

The scene was grim, clad in darkness, reeking of envy and betrayal. Crane lay sprawled in his own fortress, plastered against the cold steel. The safe was locked tight, but the question screamed loud: who twisted the key in a place where the pickings were impossible?

I knelt beside the body, my left hand quaking as I tucked a stray lock behind my ear. I couldn’t afford to lie here; the stiff made a better story than I ever could. I pushed the brittle hair back, revealing the calm on his face—like he was napping under a street lamp at midnight.

“Only thing we have is this,” Hargrove said, tossing a damp matchbook onto the grim floor. My eyes zeroed in on the embossed letters—“BLUE LAGOON.” 

“Where’s this joint?” I muttered, feeling the drain of curiosity ricochet in my noggin. 

“Not important, Slate. What’s vital is the sealed envelope clutching his right hand—let’s pry that open.”

The lifeless grip loosened, revealing a smear of red sealing wax, the kind that belonged to only the fanciest of correspondence. As I stood, I felt the weight of the matchbook pulling at my gut.

“Here’s a man who’s found in a lockbox, a wax seal, and a matchbook from a joint only crooks know,” I mused aloud. “Keep this to yourself, Hargrove. It’s not a game for a cheap whiskey.”

“Just don’t strain your good eye, Slate. Let’s solve this before the fog rolls in.”

I took one last drag from my crooked cigarette and headed out into the dreary night, knowing chaos was just a heartbeat away.

### Chapter 2

The next day was draped in fog thick enough to choke a ghost. The city was in a terrible mood, and it wasn’t going to get any better as I headed toward “Blue Lagoon.” It was a dive that hosted too many secrets and not enough daylight.

I walked into the place, my left hand shaking with every lie I contemplated. The waitress behind the bar was flipping through her shift like a deck of cards, and I slid her a fifty. “What do you know about Lionel Crane?”

She looked up, eyes narrowing. “He was here, but he didn’t leave a footprint in the sand. Too clean for this joint.”

“You know the score, doll. I’m looking for the kind of scum he tangled with,” I pressed, trying to make my unsteady hand look casual.

“Last I heard, he was whispering with Captain Rourke on the docks. Funny how secrets drown over a stiff drink,” she said, leaning closer, the tension palpable. 

Captain Rourke—his name wasn’t new on the snitch list. An old sea dog known for navigating both tides and treachery—all for a few pieces of silver.

As I was about to leave, the waitress pulled me back. “Wait! You might wanna know—” Her eyes flitted to the side, and she handed me something small. “Found this in the back. Thought you’d be interested.”

I turned it over in my palm. It was a weathered token stamped with a familiar insignia of Rourke’s ship. 

NEW CLUE: A token with the insignia of Captain Rourke.

I left the Blue Lagoon with more questions than answers. There were waves crashing underneath the surface, and it was high tide for truths to come out.

### Chapter 3

The docks were busy with the hustle of men who knew the saltwater better than any woman’s heart. As I stepped into the daylight, the stench of betrayal wafted through the air, thicker than a sailor’s breath. It all fell together like pieces of a broken watch.

I found Rourke polishing some brass on a ship, looking as innocent as a newborn. “Slate,” he called out, feigning surprise. “What winds bring you here?”

I glanced at my trembling hand, forcing it to stillness. “Just wanted to talk about a certain watchmaker.”

His face turned stormy, and I pressed harder. “You didn’t think we wouldn’t catch on, Captain? Insurance fraud doesn't float by unnoticed.”

Rourke laughed, but the sound twisted like a dull knife in my gut. “You’re barking up the wrong mast, Slate.”

“Crane locked in a box like a trapped rat,” I gestured. “But it wasn’t just luck getting him there. He had paperwork slipped inside the safe, and you rigged the locks from the outside. Your little token was a key to his secret, huh?”

Realization dawned on Rourke, his facade cracking like an old hull. “So you did figure it out,” he spat, but it was too late for him, the truth already casting its shadow.

“You used your own sealing wax to disguise the insurance policy and matchbook without ever stepping inside—two locks in one fell swoop. Easy escape while keeping the numbers in your favor,” I concluded, feeling the adrenaline shiver through my body as the pieces connected.

“Too bad you won’t live to tell the tale,” he sneered, pulling a knife that gleamed with malevolence.

But I was quicker, firing a round that rang out like a judgment day bell. Rourke crumpled, the weight of his self-made empire crashing down with him.

That’s the trouble with secrets; they never stay buried well. I walked away from the dock, my left hand shaking not from deception this time, but from having brushed too close to death and lived to tell the tale.

As the city faded into the distance, I flicked my cigarette into the waves. In a world full of lost timepieces, I’d just caught the greatest thief of them all.
