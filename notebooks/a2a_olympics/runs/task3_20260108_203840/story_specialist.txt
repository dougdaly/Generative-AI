Chapter 1  
The rain drummed on the pavement like a drummer with a grudge. Sam Slate leaned against the grimy window of his dimly lit office, smoke curling around him like a lover's embrace. He had a bad feeling about this case the moment the dame walked in, her eyes glistening with unshed tears. Lionel Crane, the watchmaker, had been found dead in the city’s most secure walk-in safe, locked tight from the outside. Folks whispered impossible, but in this town, impossible was just another word for trouble.

The crime scene had been pristine, the kind of spot that made you wonder if someone had been cleaning up ghosts. A single damp matchbook caught his eye, half-opened to reveal the words “BLUE LAGOON” smeared on the front. He could taste the inevitable bitterness in his mouth—what was a watchmaker doing at a place like that? 

As Slate dug deeper, he could feel the familiar tremor in his left hand. It shook like a leaf in a storm whenever he tried to lie. Funny how the truth could rattle a man's bones. He pocketed the matchbook like it was a hot lead, his mind already churning out theories like a madman on the typewriter. 

NEW CLUE: The damp matchbook labeled “BLUE LAGOON.”  

Chapter 2  
A few days later, Slate made his way to the docks, the stench of saltwater and secrets hitting him like a runaway train. The harbor was alive with the grumbles of heavy machinery and the whispers of men who knew too much. He had an appointment with Captain Rourke, the harbor master—respected like the old clock on the wall but ticking down to something sinister.

Rourke stood big and broad as a battleship, with eyes that glinted like the hull of a dredging tug. Slate asked him about Crane’s death, watching for any sign of a twitch or a tell. The captain spun a tale of smuggling rings and insurance scams with a slickness that could slip through your fingers. But it was the way his hand brushed against the corner of his desk that caught Slate’s attention, revealing a peculiar smear of red wax.

With the matchbook and the wax, a pattern began to form in the murky corners of Slate’s mind. He excused himself with a nod, letting the unease sit like a stone in his gut. The answer was hiding deep in the shadows, and he was determined to drag it into the light. 

NEW CLUE: A smear of red sealing wax found on Captain Rourke’s desk.  

Chapter 3  
The walls of the industrial complex closed in on Slate as he pieced the puzzle together. The keys to the safe were one thing, but how could Rourke lock the door securely while ensuring Crane never stepped out? As he stood in front of the gruesome mausoleum of metal, the answer struck him like a low-life crook in an alley. 

Rourke had used Crane’s own craftsmanship against him—attaching a small device, setting it to lockdown mode with the last tick of the man's precious watch. That red wax? It was crafted from Rourke’s own seal, an insurance claim he’d filed under the table, double-crossing his way to a smuggler's fortune. And the matchbook, well, it was a breadcrumb leading straight to his ties with the seedy underbelly of the waterfront. 

Slate felt the tremor in his hand as he calculated the lies he'd have to tell to get the proof—he could do it, but it would cost him. He couldn’t bear the shake now; he needed to confront Rourke. 

The pieces aligned, and the impossible had become a brutal reality: a man trapped into a clockwork system of deceit, all for a handful of green. In this city, everyone had a watch, and time was always running out. 

NEW CLUE: Rourke used a device with a lockdown mode built into Crane’s watch to secure the safe.
