# SMB Churn Spike – Q1 FY25 Retrospective
--- Slide 1 ---

## Context

In Q1 FY25 we increased prices on our SMB Online self-serve plans. The goal was to:

- Simplify packaging,
- Reduce discounting,
- Lift ARPU by 8–10% for new and existing small-business customers.

Immediately after rollout, we observed a spike in SMB churn.

--- Slide 2 ---

## What Happened

(Chart: Weekly SMB churn – Online self-serve, not exported)

Key observations:

1. Cancellations spiked in the 3 weeks following the price change.
2. The spike is concentrated in:
   - Month-to-month plans,
   - Customers acquired via heavy promo codes 6–12 months ago,
   - Lower product tiers.

Retail and Enterprise churn remained within normal ranges during the same period.

--- Slide 3 ---

## Root Causes (Hypotheses)

- Price-sensitive cohorts reacted strongly to the higher list prices and removal of legacy discounts.
- Messaging on the new pricing page emphasized "premium" positioning but did not clearly communicate the value for smaller accounts.
- We did not proactively notify legacy promo cohorts, so the price jump felt abrupt.

--- Slide 4 ---

## What Is Probably Just Noise

- Seasonal factors (Q1 is historically a softer quarter for SMB in our category).
- A small spike in downgrades among mid-market customers that reverted the following month.

These effects appear within historical variance bands and are not considered structural issues.

--- Slide 5 ---

## Next Steps

- Add targeted retention offers for the most price-sensitive SMB cohorts.
- Improve pricing-page copy for small-business buyers.
- Track SMB Online churn weekly for the next 2 quarters and compare against pre-change baselines.

We will consider a partial rollback or tier re-introduction if SMB churn does not normalize by the end of Q3 FY25.
