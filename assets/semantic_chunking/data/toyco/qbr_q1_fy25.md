# ToyCo – Q1 FY25 Quarterly Business Review
--- Slide 1 ---

## Overview

Q1 FY25 was mixed. Total revenue increased, but SMB churn spiked following the April price increase in our self-serve Online plans.

(Chart: Revenue vs churn – not included in export)

--- Slide 2 ---

## Revenue by Segment

Segment      Q1 FY24    Q1 FY25    Δ YoY
-----------------------------------------
Enterprise   120.1      128.6      +7.1%
SMB           45.9       44.3      -3.5%

Enterprise revenue continued to grow at a healthy pace with strong renewals and expansions. SMB revenue declined year-over-year as elevated churn offset new logo growth.

The decline is concentrated in **self-serve Online SMB customers**, particularly in lower-priced tiers.

--- Slide 3 ---

## Channel Performance

Channel      Q1 FY24    Q1 FY25    Δ YoY
-----------------------------------------
Online        81.0       89.7      +10.7%
Retail        85.0       83.2      -2.1%

Online revenue increased double digits and now represents the majority of total revenue. However, the growth is partially driven by short-term uplift from the April pricing change.

Retail volumes were slightly down as we deprioritized low-margin retail channels.

--- Slide 4 ---

## Churn & Retention

Metric                           Q1 FY24     Q1 FY25
-----------------------------------------------------
Enterprise churn                  6.0%        5.9%
SMB churn (overall)              13.5%       17.8%
SMB churn – Online self-serve    14.2%       21.3%

We implemented a **price increase for SMB Online self-serve plans** in early April. Immediately following the change, we saw:

- Higher **short-term revenue per customer**,
- A sharp spike in cancellations from price-sensitive small businesses,
- Increased downgrade activity from annual to monthly terms.

This pattern is most pronounced in customers acquired via paid performance marketing.

--- Slide 5 ---

## Commentary

While the price action improved ARPU, the associated churn spike raises questions about long-term LTV/CAC for small-business customers. 

We are monitoring whether the elevated SMB churn is a one-time reset or a new baseline. Early July data suggests churn is trending down but not yet back to pre-increase levels.

(See separate document: "SMB Churn Spike – Q1 FY25 Retrospective" for deeper analysis.)

--- Slide 6 ---

## Key Takeaways

- Enterprise remains stable and continues to grow ~7% YoY.
- Online channel revenue is up, but partially inflated by pricing changes.
- SMB churn spike following the April price increase is the main anomaly this quarter.
